busabunga.com

Type

Website build + technical SEO

Date

Launched June 2026

Role

Research, design, build, SEO

Client

PT Tjikko Sentral Industrial

A commercial site for Tjikko's floral foam line, built end to end in Next.js (React) with Claude Code as the development partner. Keyword research, information architecture, build, content and search strategy were all mine. There was no existing site, no legacy traffic, no backlink profile and no advertising budget, so the only lever available was getting the structure and the search intent right before anything shipped.

What I built

  • 16 target keywords grouped into four intent clusters (product, supplier, wholesale, private label), each mapped to a page before design started
  • Information architecture organised around buyer intent rather than the internal product catalogue
  • Full Next.js build, AI-assisted through Claude Code and Git, deployed on Vercel behind Cloudflare
  • Schema markup: Organization, WebSite, FAQPage, ItemList
  • Dedicated pages for florists, wholesale buyers and private-label enquiries
  • WhatsApp-first enquiry flow, matching how Indonesian B2B buyers actually make contact
  • Search Console and Google Business Profile set up from day one, not retrofitted
  • Post-launch Screaming Frog crawl: 100% of internal URLs indexable, none blocked
  • A daily AI SEO audit, run by Hermes, that flags issues with a reason and a fix

The challenge

A brand-new domain competing for commercial terms that established suppliers and the big marketplaces already owned. No authority to borrow from, no history for Google to trust, and no paid support to buy time while rankings matured. On a domain like that, most of the usual SEO levers are unavailable: what is left is structure, intent matching, and not making the mistakes that keep new sites invisible.

How I approached it

I mapped the commercial keyword set first and let it decide the site architecture, which is the opposite of the usual order. Primary terms were floral foam, wet and dry floral foam, busa bunga and floral foam indonesia; secondary terms covered supplier, distributor, grosir and private-label variations. Each target term got exactly one page that clearly owned it, so the site never competed with itself. Buyer-intent language went into headings and body copy rather than internal product naming, and the enquiry path was kept short enough that arriving traffic had somewhere obvious to go.

Result

Within roughly one month of launch the site held #1 and page-one Google positions for its target commercial keywords, including floral foam, wet floral foam and busa bunga, ranking above the marketplace listings. That came from structure and intent matching, not from authority the domain had not had time to build. It is the cleanest evidence I have that the method is repeatable: same approach, no inherited advantages, measurable outcome.

B2B Meta Ads + CRM

Type

Performance marketing

Date

2026

Role

Meta Ads, tracking, dashboard, AI analysis

Client

B2B corporate gifting company (anonymised)

A social enterprise selling handcrafted corporate gifts ran Meta Click-to-WhatsApp ads. In Ads Manager the ads looked efficient. Sales kept receiving chats asking about trousers, motorbike spare parts and uniforms. I set up and optimised the account, and built the measurement layer that ties every ad to what Sales actually marked in the CRM.

What I built

  • Meta Ads setup and optimisation: 10 active ads, one ad per ad set, six B2B audiences
  • An ad naming taxonomy (month, audience code, creative brief) used as the join key across every system
  • A Google Sheet tracker joining Meta spend and chats with Mekari Qontak CRM leads, per ad, per day
  • Coefficient pulling Meta data every morning
  • Ads Monitor: a Google Apps Script dashboard showing which ad produces qualified leads, which passed the cut-off without one, and where Meta actually spent
  • A daily Claude report: observation, why it matters, action, metric to watch, per ad
  • Business rules written into the prompt: cut-off, scaling steps, scepticism towards Meta’s own recommendations

The challenge

Meta counts conversations started, not leads. For premium B2B gifts a chat means little until Sales marks it relevant, and that verdict lived in the CRM while spend lived in Meta. Ad names drifted between periods so the two never matched, the CEO’s cut-off rule was being applied to chats rather than qualified leads, and ads set to Instagram were quietly serving on WhatsApp Status and Facebook Reels.

How I approached it

Sales is the source of truth: a lead is qualified only when Sales marks it, and neither the system nor the AI guesses. A strict naming taxonomy let Meta, the CRM, the dashboard and the AI refer to the same ad. Placement setting and actual delivery are tracked separately so leakage shows up. The AI reads the data every morning and recommends KEEP, SCALE, FIX or PAUSE; I review each one and make the change in Ads Manager myself.

Result

Over 1 May to 15 October 2026 the tracker traced 105 CRM-qualified leads, out of 2,573 WhatsApp chats, back to individual ads, plus three deals worth Rp167M. The joined data changed real decisions: one creative brief produced qualified leads in all five audiences, WhatsApp Status turned out to deliver the cheapest chats and none of the useful ones, and an ad that looked expensive per lead had in fact produced a Rp112M deal. Client data is anonymised.

  • Click-to-WhatsApp
  • Mekari Qontak

Date

2026 — ongoing

Role

Architect & builder

Client

Self-hosted / internal

A self-hosted multi-agent system that does real daily work. Not a demo, not a prototype. Claude API handles the reasoning, n8n owns scheduling and orchestration, and MCP connects the whole thing to live WordPress sites and analytics properties. It runs on my own VPS, which means I also own the uptime, the credentials and the failure modes.

What I built

  • Multi-agent framework on a self-hosted VPS, with a reusable skill library
  • Claude API as the reasoning engine; Claude Code and Git as the development workflow
  • n8n for scheduling, orchestration, retries and error handling
  • Claude Desktop with MCP connected to live WordPress sites
  • RAG architecture for grounding agents in real documents rather than model memory
  • Full multi-page client website builds executed through MCP
  • SEO, GEO and AEO documentation systems
  • GA4 audits and a daily reporting agent across three properties

The challenge

Most AI automation stops at the proof-of-concept stage: impressive in a screenshot, unreliable the moment it has to run unattended against production systems. The hard part was never the model call. It is everything around it: scheduling, credential handling, retry logic, and knowing when the output is wrong before it reaches anyone who would act on it.

How I approached it

I built Hermes as infrastructure rather than as a collection of scripts. Agents are composable skills with a Git workflow behind them, so behaviour is versioned and reviewable. MCP handles the integrations into live systems so agents act through real interfaces instead of scraped ones. n8n owns scheduling and retries, which keeps the failure handling out of the prompts. The design bias throughout is towards systems that degrade visibly rather than silently.

Result

A GA4 Daily Reporter runs in production across three properties, pulling the data, formatting the report and delivering it to Discord without a human in the loop. A daily SEO audit job for busabunga.com runs alongside it, reporting what works and then each issue with why it matters and a fix. The system is part of a 90-day practitioner sprint aimed at three to five production agents and a monetisable automation service.

Quotation vs PO Validation

Type

Business process automation

Date

Live since July 2026

Role

Architect & builder

Client

PT Tjikko Sentral Industrial

A first-line AI validation layer that checks incoming customer purchase orders against the quotations they came from. It is a small, unglamorous problem that quietly costs a lot: a mismatch caught after acceptance becomes a margin loss or an argument, and catching them by hand is exactly the kind of work that gets skipped when the week is busy.

What I built

  • Line-item validation: does the PO match what was actually quoted
  • Price validation: does the PO price match the quotation price
  • Expiry validation: was the quotation still valid when the PO arrived
  • Built on Claude scheduled tasks with MCP integrations into Google Drive and Gmail
  • Reports hourly, so a mismatch surfaces the same working day
  • Deliberately separate from Hermes, which is n8n-based: different failure modes, different operational risk

The challenge

Manual reconciliation is the sort of task that is important but never urgent, so it slips. It also has to be right: a validation layer that raises false alarms gets ignored within a week, and one that misses real mismatches is worse than nothing because it creates false confidence.

How I approached it

I kept the scope narrow on purpose. Three checks, clearly defined, running on a schedule rather than trying to be a general-purpose document agent. It reads from the systems the team already uses via MCP rather than asking anyone to change how they work, and reports hourly so a problem surfaces while the paperwork is still fresh. Building it separately from Hermes was a reliability decision, not a technical one: I did not want a workflow failure in one system to take out the other.

Result

Live in production since July 2026, removing a recurring manual reconciliation task from the Marketing Manager and returning that time to field and market work. It is still early, so there is deliberately no catch-rate or time-saved figure here: those numbers are worth having only once the system has a real track record behind it.

Stack

  • Claude API
  • Claude scheduled tasks
  • MCP
  • Google Drive
  • Gmail

Process

How I work
on a project

The same eight steps on every project, from the first call to aftercare. Every task lives on a shared Trello board, so you can see where things stand at any time. Click a step.

Step 01 · 30–60 minute call

Discuss your needs

We talk about the business first: what the project is for, who it has to reach, what counts as a result (an enquiry, a registration, a qualified lead), the deadline and the budget range. I look at your current site, ads or data before the call so the time goes on decisions, not on explaining.

You get

A short written summary of what I understood, so we agree on the problem before any price.

Your part

Your goals, examples you like, and access to whatever already exists.

Step 02 · A few working days

Proposal & quote

A written proposal with the scope, the deliverables, what is out of scope, the timeline, the price, the payment terms and how many revision rounds are included. If something is uncertain, the proposal says so and prices it as an option rather than hiding it.

You get

A clear proposal you can compare, forward to your team, and sign off.

Your part

Read it, ask anything, and say what to change.

Step 03 · Same week as sign-off

Agreement & kickoff

Once the proposal is approved and the first payment is in, we hold a kickoff. I collect the access I need (hosting, domain, CMS, GA4, Search Console, ad accounts) and the content you already have, and we confirm one contact person on your side for approvals.

You get

A confirmed start date and a list of what I still need from you.

Your part

Access, existing content and brand files, one decision-maker.

Step 04 · Shared at kickoff

Timeline on Trello

The project is planned on a Trello board that you can open any time: every task is a card, every milestone has a date, and cards move from Planned to In progress to Client review to Done. You always know what is happening without having to ask, and comments stay attached to the task they are about.

You get

A live Trello board with milestones and due dates.

Your part

Watch progress, comment on cards whenever you like.

Step 05 · Per the timeline

Build & execute

Design, build, SEO, tracking or ads set-up, depending on the project. Work happens on a staging link or in draft so nothing breaks your live site, and GA4 and Search Console are wired in before launch rather than after. You get a progress update every week, with a link to see it for yourself.

You get

Weekly updates and a staging link to preview the work.

Your part

Send content on time; flag anything that changes in the business.

Step 06 · Rounds agreed in the proposal

Review & revisions

You review the work on staging. Feedback goes onto the Trello board as one card per point, so nothing gets lost in a chat thread and each fix can be checked off. Revisions follow the rounds agreed in the proposal; anything new is quoted separately before I start it, so there are no surprises on the bill.

You get

Every point addressed and ticked off on the board.

Your part

Consolidated feedback per round, from the agreed decision-maker.

Step 07 · Launch day + handover

Launch & handover

Go live, then check what matters on day one: indexing, redirects, forms and WhatsApp links, conversions in GA4, and speed. You get the handover: credentials back in your name, a short guide or video on how to update the site, and the final invoice.

You get

A live, measured site you own, plus a handover guide.

Your part

Final approval and the last payment.

Step 08 · After launch

Aftercare & growth

A support window after launch for anything that turns up. For SEO and ads retainers, a monthly report built on Search Console, GA4 and CRM data, with what changed, why, and what I will do next. The findings feed straight back into step one.

You get

Support after launch and, on retainers, a monthly report.
